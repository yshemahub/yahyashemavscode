Research

Background

Salt Marsh Production Studio acquired motion capture suits in 2021. This research aimed to integrate the technology into The Bell Affair's post-production pipeline and identify applications across the studio's other projects.

Research Goals

  • Seamlessly integrate motion capture into The Bell Affair's animation pipeline.
  • Identify opportunities for scaling the technology across other studio projects.

Methodology

The research employed case studies and controlled experiments. The initial focus was assessing motion-capture retargeting techniques from Rokoko to Daz 3D MetaHuman characters. Subsequent case studies applied the resulting workflows across various studio projects to determine the optimal use cases.

Conducting Research

A select few images from our research.

Key Insights

  1. T-pose to A-pose transfer issues. Direct motion transfer from Rokoko's T-pose rig to Daz MetaHuman's A-pose rig creates significant error rates due to the pose difference, resulting in bone misalignment, joint rotation offsets, and skeletal hierarchy mismatches during retargeting.
  2. Selective workflow efficiency. Motion capture improved efficiency in specific animation shots, though the benefit diminished in sequences with minimal full-body movement, where manual keyframing and IK/FK blending often produced cleaner results with less cleanup.
  1. Unreal Engine performance. Unreal Engine and its MetaHuman characters performed exceptionally well, outperforming alternatives in diverse character creation, photorealistic rendering via Lumen and Nanite, comprehensive filmmaking capability through Sequencer, and community support.
  2. Motion capture advantages. The technology proved most beneficial for full-body movement sequences, particularly those involving complex locomotion, weight shifts, and multi-joint articulation that would be time-consuming to hand-animate.
